[FIXED 2026-09-12] THE TUTORIAL'S FIRST BREW SCORED MEDIOCRE QUALITY
================================================================
File: src/classes/brew_resolver.gd (resolve_brew_style / _range_precision),
src/resources/beer_styles/kotikalja.tres

Played the tutorial exactly as designed: bought 3kg Pilsner malt + 1
baking yeast (the pre-seeded Kotikalja default recipe), put them on the
table, brewed. Result: 38 bottles at quality 0.8 — NOT the 1.0
"original_quality" a new player would reasonably expect from following
the game's own guidance.

Why: Kotikalja intentionally has no real EBC/IBU bounds (it's the
catch-all fallback style), so it falls back to the BeerStyle class
defaults of min_ebc=0/max_ebc=999 and min_ibu=0/max_ibu=999. That's NOT
treated as "wide open, so score it as perfect" — _range_precision() scores
distance from the range's CENTER (~500 EBC, ~500 IBU), and a real minimal
Kotikalja batch resolves to ~2 EBC / 0 IBU. That's about as far from 500
as you can get, so precision_score comes out near 0, dragging the
quality_multiplier down to ~0.8.

This means literally every new player's first, safest, most
by-the-book brew comes out looking like a mistake. First impressions
matter most for hooking a player — I'd either give Kotikalja tight
EBC/IBU bounds of its own (matching what its default recipe actually
produces), or special-case the fallback style's precision scoring.

Fixed: gave Kotikalja a tight max_ebc=4 (min_ebc stays 0), centered
exactly on the default recipe's real output (Pilsner malt, EBC 2) —
EBC precision now scores 1.0, landing the overall quality_multiplier at
exactly 1.0 (IBU precision still scores 0 at the min_ibu=0 edge for a
hop-free brew, but that's now the neutral point of the formula, not a
penalty). IBU bounds deliberately left untouched (still 0-999) so
Kotikalja keeps its role as the catch-all fallback for odd ingredient
combos — a combo whose EBC now falls outside the tightened window just
routes through the hardcoded is_matched=false fallback instead, which
already flatly quality=1.0. Verified live: the exact tutorial recipe (3kg
Pilsner malt + 1 baking yeast) now brews at quality 1.0.
src/resources/beer_styles/kotikalja.tres.


ECONOMY BALANCE (post pricing/tax/tip rework)
===============================================
[NOTE 2026-09-12] Excise duty and VAT were removed from pricing entirely
after this report — a hidden cellar operation doesn't remit anything to
the state, so there was never a real "tax" to model (see
src/classes/brew_resolver.gd's calculate_price_breakdown()). Price is
now just raw_cost + profit margin, no markup for tax, and
CustomerManager no longer deducts anything at sale time (gross_income +
tip = net_income, full stop). The specific EUR figures and the tip
rounding-floor numbers below are now stale (prices dropped roughly
40-55%) — the qualitative shape (cheap styles cost little, complex
styles earn proportionally more via profit_margin_multiplier) still
holds, the absolute numbers don't.

Verified live via BrewResolver.get_price_breakdown() for 6 representative
styles (price assumes cheapest valid recipe; batch = 38 bottles after the
5% bottling loss) — figures below are from BEFORE the tax removal, kept
for the qualitative comparison, see note above:

  Style            Price/bottle   Batch ingredient cost   Batch gross revenue
  Kotikalja        1.16 EUR       13.70 EUR                44.17 EUR
  Bulkkilager      2.10 EUR       26.70 EUR                79.81 EUR
  Pale Ale         2.98 EUR       42.70 EUR               113.21 EUR
  IPA              5.20 EUR       72.70 EUR               197.48 EUR
  Imperial Stout   7.21 EUR       91.70 EUR               274.16 EUR
  Barleywine       7.26 EUR       90.70 EUR               275.79 EUR

Observations:
- Starting money is 100 EUR (src/classes/brewery.gd:17). Nothing gates a
  brand-new player from immediately dumping ~90% of it into one Imperial
  Stout or Barleywine batch turn one — no reputation/discovery lock on
  ingredients or brewing, only on which CUSTOMERS will buy it (see below).
  That's a real, interesting risk/reward choice right at the start of the
  game (go safe with Kotikalja, or gamble the whole bankroll on a
  high-value style you can't yet reliably sell) — I'd call this a
  strength, not a gap, as long as it's intentional.
- Daily overhead (electricity+water = 8 EUR/day, src/autoload/
  time_manager.gd:14-15, gated behind tutorial_complete()) is trivial
  next to even a single Kotikalja batch's ~11-12 EUR margin. It never
  becomes a real threat once the player is brewing at all — right now
  it's flavor rather than a lever. Could scale with reputation/day count
  if you want upkeep to matter later game.
- The new profit_margin_multiplier (1.0x base styles up to 1.5x for
  Imperial Stout/Barleywine) plus the ABV-driven excise math means
  higher-effort styles are unambiguously more profitable per bottle AND
  per euro of ingredients invested (Kotikalja ~3.2x markup on cost vs.
  Barleywine's built-in 1.5x profit multiplier on top of that same
  ratio). Clean, legible progression incentive — a player who tastes
  Barleywine's economics once has an obvious reason to keep pushing into
  harder styles.

Tip mechanic (new): verified with a manually-quality-controlled IPA sale
— masterful quality (1.8, customer bar 0.5, margin 1.3) produced a 2 EUR
tip on a 5 EUR sale (a real, felt bonus); the identical batch sold at bad
quality (0.2) produced 0 EUR tip, same 5 EUR base price either way (price
truly doesn't move — confirmed the "same style, same price" rule holds
regardless of who's buying or how good the batch is). Also confirmed
price is identical for a matched-primary-style customer and a
wrong-style customer buying the exact same batch — style-match no longer
touches price.

However: at cheap styles' price points (Kotikalja ~1 EUR, Bulkkilager ~2
EUR), a "clearly exceeds expectations" quality margin of 0.5 with the
default customer's quality_tip_sensitivity (0.3) rounds the tip down to
0 EUR every time (roundi(1 * 0.5 * 0.3) = 0). Verified directly — three
separate test sales at quality margin 0.5 on a ~1-3 EUR base price all
tipped 0. Tips only become visible once quality margin and/or price get
large enough to clear the rounding floor (my masterful-quality IPA test
did; a merely-good Kotikalja sale won't). Since most of a new player's
early sales ARE cheap styles, "you might get more tip" as a felt
mechanic is effectively invisible for the first stretch of play. Worth
either giving tip a small minimum floor when margin > 0, or accepting
it's meant to read as a late-game/high-value-style reward only.

[FIXED 2026-09-12] Added CustomerData.MIN_TIP_PER_QUALITY_POINT (1 EUR
per full quality point of margin) as a price-independent floor under the
proportional formula: tip = max(income*margin*sensitivity,
margin*MIN_TIP_PER_QUALITY_POINT) * budget_multiplier. The proportional
term still wins (and keeps scaling with price) once income is high enough
to clear the flat floor on its own, so expensive styles aren't
undercut — verified a masterful IPA sale still tips proportionally to
price, not just the flat floor. Verified live: a Kotikalja sale at
quality margin 0.5 (previously 0 EUR tip) now tips 1 EUR.
src/classes/customer_data.gd.


RISK / AVI RAID
================
Raid trigger: src/classes/brewery.gd:113-128 — risk >= 100
(AVI_RAID_THRESHOLD) confiscates the ENTIRE current inventory, takes a
30% cash fine (AVI_RAID_FINE_PERCENT) and a 25% reputation cut
(AVI_RAID_REPUTATION_PENALTY_PERCENT), then resets risk to 0. Triggered
inline every time Brewery.add_risk() is called — i.e. it can fire
mid-sale, not just at day boundaries.

Verified live: with a fresh 20-bottle batch in inventory (real ingredient
investment) and risk pushed to 100, the raid took 29 EUR, 5 reputation,
and all 20 bottles in one instant. There is no player-facing warning
beyond src/autoload/audio_manager.gd's tension drone, which scales
volume by risk/100 — a nice diegetic touch, but it's the ONLY signal; no
UI alert, no "last chance" moment, no confirmation. For a first-time
player who hasn't connected "the ambient audio is getting worse" to "I'm
about to lose everything," this will land as a sudden, unexplained
gut-punch rather than earned tension.

DailyGoalsPanel's own risk goal sits well below the actual cliff
(RISK_LIMIT=50 vs. raid at 100) with a modest reward (10 EUR/2
reputation) for staying under it — a sensible soft-warning layer in
principle, but nothing in the UI communicates that crossing 100 isn't
"a bit worse than missing the goal," it's "lose your entire stock
instantly." I'd add an explicit threshold warning (a color change, an
icon, a one-time toast) somewhere around risk 75-90, on top of the audio.

[FIXED 2026-09-12] Added a RISK_WARNING_THRESHOLD=75 in
top_panel_resources.gd, between the 50 goal and the 100 cliff: the "AVI
Riski" label tints to the same failing-red used elsewhere in the UI for
as long as risk stays at or above it, and pulses once on the moment it
first crosses upward (not on every subsequent update, and not looping —
a one-time "heads up" beat, not a nag). Clears back to normal the moment
risk drops back under 75. Verified live: pushing risk to 77 applied the
tint and pulsed once; dropping it to 10 cleared the tint.

Per-sale risk varies meaningfully by which customer you're serving —
worth calling out since it's a nice, currently-invisible bit of depth:
Agentti (the AVI-inspector-as-customer archetype, unlocks at 50
reputation) carries primary_match_risk_multiplier=2.5 — serving their
preferred style is riskier than almost anything else in the game.
Mafioso carries -1.5 on the same field, actively REDUCING risk on a
primary-style match — thematically it reads as "protection," and
mechanically it makes Mafioso sales a genuine risk-management tool once
unlocked. Neither of these is explained anywhere in the UI; a player
would have to infer it from repeated play.


CUSTOMER / REPUTATION UNLOCK CURVE
====================================
  Min. reputation   Archetypes unlocked         Notes
  0 (start)         Opiskelija, Raksamies, Zgen  Forgiving quality bars (0.05-0.3)
  15                Leijonafani                  Still forgiving (0.05), randomized preference
  20                Barbaari, Hipsteri           Hipsteri is the first real quality test (bar 0.8)
  50                Agentti, Mafioso             Agentti = risk-heavy; Mafioso = big tipper/spender, risk-reducer

A single well-matched sale grants roughly +11 to +15 reputation in my
tests (default customer, decent-quality Kotikalja). At that rate the
ENTIRE customer roster — all 8 archetypes — can unlock inside one
session well under an hour, often much faster once the reputation-spawn
feedback loop below kicks in. For a short/casual session that's fine;
for anyone who plays longer, there's currently nothing left to unlock
past 50 reputation — no new mechanic, no new customer tier, nothing.
That's the single biggest lever I'd pull for long-session
addictiveness: stretch the curve further (a 100+ tier) or attach
something else to reputation milestones (new ingredients, a shop
upgrade, a cosmetic) so late-game still has a "just a bit more" pull.

[PARTIALLY ADDRESSED 2026-09-12] Did not add a new discrete unlock tier
(new customer archetypes need new art/sprites — out of scope for a
balance pass) or a new milestone-reward system. Instead, made sure the
mechanics that already scale continuously with reputation don't quietly
cap out: group-visit frequency is now reputation-scaled (see PACING
below) alongside the walk-in curve, so reputation past 50 keeps paying
off in busier days even with the full roster already unlocked. A real
new discrete tier is still a bigger, separate design decision — flagging
that this is a partial answer, not a full one.

The walk-in spawn-rate curve, on the other hand, is genuinely the best
hook in the game: src/classes/customer_spawner.gd:77-81 computes
factor = 100 / (100 + reputation), shrinking the walk-in interval
(30-90s baseline) as reputation climbs, floored at 10s. This is a clean,
textbook compounding loop — more fame -> busier bar -> more sales -> more
fame — and it's already well-tuned (verified the formula directly, and
felt the effect in the earlier eval-driven sales). I'd protect this
mechanic specifically in any future rebalancing; it's doing a lot of the
game's "just one more sale" work.


PACING / SESSION STRUCTURE
=============================
Day length defaults to 300s (5 minutes) real time
(src/autoload/time_manager.gd:17). Against that:

- Walk-in customers every 30-90s at 0 reputation (faster as reputation
  rises) — roughly 3-10 visits per day early, busier later. Feels
  appropriately busy for a 5-minute day.
- Group visits fire every 240-480s (src/classes/customer_spawner.gd:10-11)
  and are NOT reputation-scaled. The average interval (360s) is longer
  than a full default day (300s) — meaning most days will see zero group
  visits by pure chance, and the feature (confirmed working well,
  crowd-formation and all, in the last report) is statistically
  under-exposed relative to how polished it is. I'd either shorten this
  interval or scale it down with reputation the way walk-ins already are.

  [FIXED 2026-09-12] Halved the base interval to 150-300s (avg 225s, now
  under the default day length) and applied the exact same
  reputation-scaling factor walk-ins already use, floored at a still-rarer
  120s (vs. walk-ins' 10s floor) so groups stay a "special" event even at
  high reputation. This also directly helps the "nothing scales past 50
  reputation" finding below — group-visit frequency keeps improving well
  past that point. Verified: factor/interval computed correctly at
  reputation 0 (150-300s), 50 (120-200s), and 100 (120-150s, floor
  reached). src/classes/customer_spawner.gd.

- Both walk-in and group-visit timers silently skip and reschedule if
  brewery.inventory.brew_batches is empty — there's no "the bar is empty,
  come back later" signal for the player, customers just... stop coming.
  Combined with the day timer, a player who spends too long fiddling with
  the brew screen on a given day can burn most of it with nobody walking
  in at all, with no feedback explaining why. A simple "no beer to sell!"
  indicator would close this gap cheaply.

  [FIXED 2026-09-12] BeerBatchPanel now shows "Ei olutta myytävänä — keitä
  lisää!" in place of the (previously just empty) batch list whenever
  inventory.brew_batches has nothing left to sell. Verified live.
  scenes/ui/beer_batch_panel.gd.


BEER STYLE / SHELF-LIFE DESIGN
=================================
Complexity scales sensibly across three axes at once: min_malt_weight
(3kg -> 5kg), ABV (2.8% -> 10%), and profit_margin_multiplier (1.0x ->
1.5x) all climb together from Kotikalja up through Imperial
Stout/Barleywine. That's a clear, well-communicated "invest more, earn
proportionally more (and then some)" ladder.

Shelf life runs the opposite direction from what I'd have guessed: the
priciest, biggest-investment styles (Barleywine, Imperial Stout) also
get the longest shelf life (900 days, 60-day peak) — so the styles with
the most money on the line are also the safest to hold unsold. Cheaper
styles (Pale Ale, IPA, Hefeweizen, Witbier, Session Ale) spoil faster
(14-45 days) with negative aging factors. In practice, though, 14 days =
about 70 minutes of real play at the default day length, so spoilage
essentially never becomes a felt concern within a normal session — it's
a mechanic that's present and internally consistent, but not currently
pulling any weight in day-to-day decisions. Worth knowing it's inert
rather than assuming it's tuning pressure.


ADDICTIVENESS VERDICT
========================
Strengths:
- The reputation -> spawn-rate feedback loop (customer_spawner.gd) is a
  genuine, well-tuned compounding hook — the single strongest piece of
  the loop.
- Reputation-gated customer tiers reward early progress on a fast,
  satisfying cadence (0/15/20/50).
- The new pricing engine gives real, legible stakes to the
  cheap-and-safe vs. expensive-and-ambitious brewing choice, and the new
  per-customer risk multipliers (Agentti/Mafioso) add depth that rewards
  paying attention to who you're serving.
- DailyGoalsPanel's near-miss pulse animation on the bottles goal is
  exactly the kind of "just one more sale" nudge that makes a loop
  compulsive, and it's already implemented well.
- The tip mechanic adds a small unexpected-reward moment on top of
  expected income — good dopamine shape, pending the cheap-style
  rounding-to-zero issue noted above.

Friction that worked against the loop (status as of 2026-09-12):
- [FIXED] No explicit AVI raid warning beyond ambient audio — a first
  raid was likely to feel like an unfair surprise rather than earned
  tension. Now tints/pulses the risk label at risk >= 75.
- [FIXED] Group visits were statistically under-exposed relative to day
  length. Now reputation-scaled and a shorter baseline interval.
- [PARTIALLY ADDRESSED] The full customer roster unlocks inside a single
  session; group-visit frequency now keeps scaling past that point, but
  no new discrete content tier was added — see the unlock-curve section
  above.
- [FIXED] The tutorial's own default recipe produced a mediocre-quality
  first brew. Now scores a clean 1.0.
- [FIXED] Silent customer-spawn starvation when inventory is empty, with
  no player-facing explanation. BeerBatchPanel now says so directly.

None of the above were severe — the moment-to-moment loop (serve, earn,
watch reputation tick up, get busier, get ambitious) was already solid,
and this pass closed out four of the five friction points plus all three
carried-over bugs from the original report, without touching the
mechanics that were already working well (the reputation->spawn-rate
loop, the fixed-price economy, the daily-goal near-miss pulse). The one
remaining open item is a genuine new-content decision, not a tuning fix:
whether/how to extend progression with something past 50 reputation
beyond what group-visit scaling already covers.
